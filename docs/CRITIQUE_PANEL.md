# The Critique Panel

> Before evolving the house, five voices were asked to walk through it and speak
> plainly — each from one discipline, each blind to the others. Their verdicts
> converged with unusual force. This document preserves their words and distills
> the convergence that shaped the Design Constitution.

The panel reviewed the creative vision (`00-vision-brief.md`), the built structure,
and render screenshots of the current build in its **placeholder-photography state**
(real photography drops in later — several notes about "gradient panels" are partly
an artifact of that state, and are read here for the deeper point beneath them).

---

## 1. The Architect
*(in the lineage of Pawson, Ando, Vervoordt)*

**Resonant** — The core sequence is genuinely architectural: *door → sketch →
building → rooms* is a promenade, not a menu. "Whitespace, light, and time are
materials" is the truest line in the brief; treating *duration* as a building
material is the honest move. The refusal of stock photography — presence implied
through objects — is proper restraint.

**Weak** — "The overture reads as a *silhouette*, not a *threshold*. That dark arch
is a flat cutout floating on a beige field… a logo, not a door. A threshold requires
two rooms and a wall between them; here there is only a shape." The rooms "have
collapsed into the same tan gradient… one room relabeled eight times. Procession
dies when every space has the same proportion." Light "is described as material but
rendered as *gradient*. A gradient is decoration; light is direction."

**Directives** —
1. Set the door into a foreground limestone wall with a visible jamb and floor plane
   so arrival is a *crossing*, not a click on a silhouette.
2. Give each of the rooms a distinct proportion and ceiling height so procession is
   *felt* as changing volume, not read as changing text.
3. Replace static gradients with one directional light that advances dawn → dusk
   across the walk, and delete every drop-shadowed card.

---

## 2. The Luxury Hospitality Designer
*(Aman / Kimpton lineage)*

**Resonant** — "The overture reads as a true arrival, not a splash screen. 'We've
been expecting you.' is the single most important sentence in hospitality — the
guest who is *expected* has already stopped transacting." The absent-founder captions
are "quietly generous — the house implies *you* into the empty chair."

**Weak** — The door "renders as a flat, near-black slab… a guest feels *expected* by
the words and then *unwelcomed* by the material." "'SKIP' in the corner is a hotel
handing you the fire-exit map at check-in… cheapening the ceremony." The Table
"over-introduces itself, like a host who won't stop talking in the doorway." "Nothing
signals *how long* the wander is or where the guest is."

**Directives** —
1. Kill the "SKIP" link; let the overture auto-open after a held beat.
2. Give The Table one greeting only — lead with "The Table," drop the kicker.
3. Build a closing room that dims the light and leaves "The door stays open," so
   every guest departs feeling *kept*.

---

## 3. The Founder Who Just Exited a 20-Year Business

**Resonant** — The Podium — "Empty. Waiting for what you have become" — and Field
Note 126, "The founders who go on to build the most give the most away first," are
"the only places the guide arc stops being a slogan and becomes an object I can
feel." "'Where founders remember who they are beneath the business.' After 20 years,
that's the true one." "A house willing to write its own tiebreaker is a house with
taste."

**Weak** — "The manifesto is six sentences that all begin 'We believe.' At six,
belief becomes cadence, not conviction." "'Founder transformation ecosystem' and
'catalyst for founders building brands that matter' — this is the exact consultant
vocabulary the door is trying to escape." "The trademark on 'Founders Leading
Founders™' cheapens it. You don't ™ a philosophy you're asking me to believe is
sincere." The Stage's arc is *asserted*: "It tells me the ending before I've earned
it."

**Directives** —
1. Cut the manifesto to two lines and delete every ™ on the site.
2. Build one concrete act of giving-back — a founder leaves something a later founder
   receives — and make it the payoff of The Stage.
3. Replace "ecosystem" and "catalyst" with plain human language a tired founder would
   actually say.

---

## 4. The First-Time Founder Seeking Guidance

**Resonant** — "The door and 'Come in. We've been expecting you.' undid something in
me. I clicked expecting a pitch and got a greeting… I was expected." "'where founders
remember who they are beneath the business.' I *am* the business right now, and it's
eating me. That line saw the part of me I don't say out loud." The Blueprint "doesn't
scold me for lacking a plan — it offers me ground to stand on."

**Weak** — "After 'Come in,' I don't know what I'm walking into or what it costs…
Warmth without orientation makes me anxious." "Every room is a beautiful noun. I
can't tell which one is for a person on day one… There's no 'start here.'" "I came
looking for someone to believe in me, and I keep finding sunlight and empty chairs.
At some point I need a face and a hand."

**Directives** —
1. Add a persistent, unintimidating "New here? Start here" thread that leads a
   doubting first-timer to one clear first step.
2. Give every room a single plain-language line naming who it's for and what actually
   happens inside.
3. Introduce one real founder's voice early — someone who arrived lost — so I believe
   this house has room for me *now*, not "someday."

---

## 5. The Kinfolk Creative Director

**Resonant** — "The overture holds real stillness… This page could open an issue."
The caption-in-waiting *"a founder by a tall window, half in shadow"* is "the single
most editorial gesture here — it art-directs the absent image in words."

**Weak** — "The rooms are not yet rooms; they are *gradient cards*… a UI component —
a modal, a SaaS card — not architecture. It is the one thing the brief forbids." "The
kicker labels — HOW THE HOUSE THINKS, THE CHAPTER — over-explain. A house does not
caption its own rooms. Tracked-out all-caps is the most 'brand' reflex in the whole
piece." "'Come in.' … floats the type off the architecture rather than into it."
Mixed straight/curly apostrophes — "Kinfolk is made of small crimes avoided."

**Directives** —
1. Render each room as one photograph on flat limestone with the title in the lower
   third and ~60% empty frame; delete the soft-cornered gradient panels.
2. Demote Cormorant to display; set captions/labels in lowercase Jost; remove every
   tracked-out all-caps kicker.
3. Fix all apostrophes/quotes to typographic glyphs; move "Come in." down onto the
   threshold light so it rests *in* the architecture, not on it.

---

## The Convergence

Where independent disciplines agree, listen hardest. Seven signals recurred:

1. **The threshold must be a crossing, not a silhouette.** *(Architect, Hospitality,
   Kinfolk)* — Build a materially real oak door set in a limestone wall, the brass
   handle catching light and turning, the words resting in the light-spill.
2. **Rooms must differ — in volume, light, and framing.** *(Architect, Kinfolk)* —
   No repeated vignette; distinct proportion and a photograph on flat material with
   generous negative space, not a floating drop-shadowed card.
3. **Light is directional and tracks time.** *(Architect)* — One advancing sun,
   dawn at the door to dusk at Private Advisory.
4. **Retire the ceremony-cheapening "SKIP" and the over-introduction.** *(Hospitality,
   Kinfolk)* — Auto-open after a held beat; one greeting per room; strip tracked-caps
   kickers.
5. **Give the overwhelmed a "start here" and a felt "you are here."** *(First-timer,
   Hospitality)* — A quiet, persistent first step and light wayfinding that never
   breaks the spell.
6. **Bring a real founder's voice — and a face — forward.** *(First-timer, Exited)* —
   Surface authentic witness early, not buried in the footer.
7. **Enact the guide arc; don't assert it. And tighten the voice.** *(Exited, Kinfolk,
   First-timer)* — Make "Founders Leading Founders" a real act (a founder leaves
   something a later founder receives), cut the manifesto to its two sharpest lines,
   remove the ™s and consultant nouns from the felt experience, and fix the
   typographic small crimes.

These seven became the spine of the Design Constitution.
